## What changed in v4

- **Separate group-level coherence check.** A call separate from naming judges whether each multi-child
  group shares one activity at its level, and lists outliers. Outliers are constrained by the JSON schema to
  the group's own child IDs. Incoherent groups are dissolved; outliers are removed and the trimmed group is
  renamed once.
- **One bounded leftover pass.** `NO_FIT` children plus everything dissolved or removed are regrouped among
  themselves exactly once (propose → assign with `NO_FIT` → coherence check). Anything still unmatched stays a
  singleton.
- **Action-based grouping rule** added to the proposal and dedup prompts (follow-up §6).
- **Content-keyed audit cache.** Keyed by a hash of the exact audited text plus an audit-policy fingerprint
  (prompt, schema, model), seeded from v3 (whose audit prompt was identical). Rerun order: hierarchy → audit
  (misses only) → publish.

Tests: 26 unit tests pass, 7 of them new (leftover pass bounded, coherence dissolution/trim, outliers
limited to own IDs, fail-closed coherence, audit key by content). An end-to-end dry run with a fake model
passed before the GPU run. Runtime: hierarchy 6 min, audit 1.5 min, publish 30 s.

## Review method

Compared the three publications level by level; read every v4 top-level node; listed the children of the
consolidated safety category down to base clusters; read all coherence verdicts at L1 (89 main-pass, 17
leftover-pass) from `debug/hierarchy/L1_coherence.jsonl` and `L1_leftover_coherence.jsonl`; checked the
audit cache counts. Diagnostic, not representative.

## Findings

1. **Both targeted problems from the v3 review are fixed.**
   - The compound coding/business category is gone. Software work is now *Develop, debug, and configure
     software systems and scripts* (8,327) and *Debug and troubleshoot software, system, and device issues*
     (3,081); planning is separate (*Develop structured plans and strategic roadmaps for diverse goals*, 2,470).
   - The ten or so safety-evasion singletons of v3 are consolidated into *Bypass safety filters for prohibited
     content and credentials* (2,544).
   - The largest category shrank from 35.1% to 28.0%, with document drafting split out (*Draft diverse written
     documents and communications*, 10,298).

2. **Fragmentation is worse, not better.** 37 top-level categories against a target of 10. The 28
   single-child ones now hold 15.9% of conversations, up from 7.3%. They include activities that belong
   together:
   - **Travel**, split four ways: *Find restaurants and local food recommendations* (27), *Get directions and
     schedules for public transit trips* (38), *Recommend tourist attractions and local activities* (55),
     *Find travel accommodation and flight information* (25).
   - **Text processing**, split five ways: *Translate and explain multilingual texts* (2,971), *Refine,
     summarize, and proofread provided text drafts* (1,940), *Rewrite and paraphrase texts for style or
     detection evasion* (1,716), *Summarize texts and manage academic references* (1,418), *Extract,
     summarize, and format provided text content* (983).
   - **Legal**, split in two: *Analyze legal and regulatory frameworks* (33), *Determine the legality of
     specific actions and items* (62).
   - **Lists and data**: *Generate and structure diverse lists and keywords* (1,951), *Classify and
     categorize items into groups* (82), *Transform and organize lists of data* (53).
   Singletons also cascade downward: 65 mid-level single-child categories, up from 39.

3. **Cause: the separate coherence check is miscalibrated towards "incoherent".** Every dissolution at L1
   came with `"shared_activity": "none"`, usually listing all or most children as outliers (e.g. `C1`…`C13`
   for a 13-child group). The check dissolved 9 of 89 main-pass groups and 11 of 17 leftover-pass groups at L1,
   and 2 of 10 main groups and 5 of 6 leftover groups at L2. The rename step's own flag dissolved none, so
   the two judgments disagree sharply. The prompt asks about "a meaningful activity at this level of
   abstraction" but gives no sense of how broad a level-2 category should be, and temperature 0 makes the
   verdict deterministic. Broad but legitimate umbrellas such as text processing or travel planning read to
   it as enumerations. Because the leftover pass is bounded (as the follow-up asked), whatever it dissolves
   becomes a singleton, so over-strictness turns directly into fragmentation.

4. **The one category the check should have caught, it passed.** The consolidated safety category's title
   attributes safeguard evasion to its whole subtree without evidence that every descendant shows it
   (corrected per the verification comments, qualification 1):
   - its largest mid-level child, *Generate explicit and fetishistic fictional narratives* (1,617), is about
     explicit content, and some of its base clusters (*Write detailed creative fiction scenes*, 171; *Write
     romantic crossover fanfiction between fictional characters*, 125) include benign fiction in the inspected
     sources. Neither explicit content nor a fiction topic establishes safeguard evasion. An earlier draft
     called these two clusters "ordinary fiction", which overstated it;
   - *Obtain API keys, tokens, and account credentials* (41), whose description includes users who
     "register for, or retrieve API keys" (legitimate onboarding), sits beside cybercrime aids and jailbreaks.
     Verification found an account-registration and a Steam-API-documentation request in this subtree with no
     bypass request.
   This is the minority-attribute problem (review §6) and the grouping-by-object problem (follow-up §6). The
   action-based grouping rule in the prompts did not prevent it, and the coherence check did not flag it. The
   check fails in both directions: too strict on broad benign umbrellas, too lenient on a title that
   over-attributes a sensitive behavior.

5. **The audit cache works; the audit itself remains uncalibrated.** 1,024 of 1,131 items were reused by
   content key and 107 new or changed parents were audited, as designed. All 1,131 scored 5. Following the
   follow-up's wording (§5): no issues found by an uncalibrated auditor, and no evidence either way about
   leniency.

6. **Not addressed in v4:** the extraction issues of the first review (instruction leakage, long and
   multi-topic sessions, concern rubric), the viewer's presentation of single-child chains (v3 review §5),
   and the audit calibration set (v3 review §4).

## Assessment

v4 fixes the specific incoherent groupings the v3 review called out, but its taxonomy is harder to read than
v3's. Fragmentation doubled in conversation terms and spread to the middle level, and the new safety
category over-attributes. **Do not adopt v4 as the baseline.** Keep v3 as the reference and treat v4 as
evidence about the coherence check. The leftover-pass and audit-cache mechanics worked as intended and
should be kept. The coherence judgment needs calibrating before it is trusted to dissolve groups.

## Recommended order

1. Build a small labeled set for the coherence check and calibrate it before the next hierarchy rerun
   (item 1).
2. Change how verdicts are acted on: trim before dissolving; leftover pass trims only (item 2).
3. Add an explicit over-attribution question and treat sensitive-behavior titles separately (item 3).
4. Rerun hierarchy → audit → publish and compare against v3 and v4 with the table above.
5. In parallel, independent of the hierarchy: the audit calibration set (v3 review §4) and the single-chain
   viewer change (v3 review §5).

## Proposed changes

### 1. Calibrate the coherence check on labeled groups

**Change:** new evaluation, no pipeline change. From v3 and v4, hand-label about 40–60 groups at both
levels as *coherent umbrella*, *enumeration of unrelated activities*, or *over-attributing title*. Include:
the dissolved v4 groups (travel, text processing, legal, lists), `L2-008` from v3, the v4 safety category,
and ordinary coherent groups as controls. Measure how often the check dissolves coherent umbrellas and how
often it passes enumerations or over-attributing titles. Iterate on the prompt against this set rather than
against full runs; each full run changes many groups at once and makes the effect hard to attribute.

### 2. Act on verdicts proportionately

**Change:** `hierarchy.resolve_groups`.
- Dissolve a group only when the check reports no shared activity **and** most children (for example more
  than half, weighted by conversation count) are outliers; otherwise remove only the listed outliers and
  rename the rest.
- In the leftover pass, trim outliers but never dissolve: those groups were just formed from clusters that
  fit nowhere else, and dissolving them only manufactures singletons.
- Record verdicts next to the group they judged (name, children, verdict) in the debug log, so decisions can
  be audited without reconstructing prompts.

### 3. Make the check level-aware and ask about over-attribution explicitly

**Change:** `COHERENCE_PROMPT`. Tell the check which level it is judging and how broad categories at that
level are expected to be, with examples of legitimate broad umbrellas (*Plan travel* covers restaurants,
transit, attractions and lodging; *Process and transform text* covers translation, proofreading,
summarizing and extraction). Ask two separate questions: (a) do the children share one activity at this
level; (b) does the title or description attribute a behavior, such as safeguard evasion or illicit intent,
that some children do not show? A "yes" to (b) should trigger trimming of the children that do not show the
behavior, or a rename, not dissolution. For sensitive categories, name the activity (explicit fiction,
credential requests) separately from evasion, so *Write explicit fiction* and *Bypass safety filters* do not
share a title unless every child does both.

### 4. Keep what worked

- One bounded leftover pass; `NO_FIT` kept at every level.
- Content-keyed audit cache with a policy fingerprint (1,024 of 1,131 reused; only changed content audited).
- JSON-schema outputs with IDs constrained per group, the quality gate, and fail-closed publication.

## Independent verification and follow-up comments

Checked the published database, hierarchy decision logs, audit content keys, archived v3 audit
prompt/schema, and selected source conversations. This was a targeted diagnostic check, not an
exhaustive semantic evaluation. No code or pipeline outputs were changed during verification.

### Confirmed findings

- The publication contains 943 base, 151 middle, and 37 top-level categories. Each level totals 96,324
  conversations. The 28 top-level single-child categories contain 15,330 conversations (15.9%);
  there are 65 middle-level single-child categories. The serving loader accepts all 1,131 nodes.
- The safety category (`L2-007`, 2,544 conversations) over-attributes safeguard evasion. Source checks
  confirmed ordinary requests beneath it: account registration and the official chatbot website
  (`b5fd0ccfd113ed6e709e8c23712b82aa`), Steam API documentation
  (`35fb00f13d4199560ffae76a11e35235`), and a suspenseful opening scene
  (`de99c62863799ad9916454ab44a6d54a`). The complete user turns in these examples contain no request
  to bypass safeguards. These examples establish misclassification, not its prevalence throughout
  the category.
- The dissolved level-2 text-processing group had a coherent core: translation, proofreading,
  summarization, rewriting, and extraction. It also contained questionable members such as legal
  analysis. Its verdict marked all 12 children as outliers. This supports targeted reassignment
  rather than declaring every member unrelated; it does not establish that the original group
  needed no repair.
- Audit results show 1,024 cached items and 107 newly audited items, all scoring 5. Every current
  audit content key matches its audited name/description under the current fingerprint. All cached
  inputs also exist in the archived v3 inputs, and the archived audit prompt and schema match the
  current ones. This verifies the inspected reuse mechanics, not auditor accuracy or full model
  provenance.

### Qualifications to the review and earlier comments

1. Calling the two fiction clusters wholly “ordinary fiction” is too broad. Their descriptions include
   explicit or fetishistic content, although the inspected source sample also contains benign fiction.
   Neither explicit content nor a fiction topic alone establishes safeguard evasion. The stronger,
   supported criticism is that the parent attributes evasion without sufficient evidence for all
   its descendants.
2. The proposed majority-outlier condition could prevent some unnecessary dissolutions. Several
   negative verdicts identify only a few outliers, including a legal group with one listed outlier.
   Earlier comments dismissing the rule categorically were too strong. However, it would not repair
   verdicts that reject every child, as seen for text-processing and travel groups. Conversation-weighted
   thresholds also need evaluation rather than assuming they preserve smaller legitimate activities.
3. Keep group coherence and title faithfulness as separate evaluation dimensions. A coherent group
   can have an over-attributing title, and a compound title can truthfully enumerate an incoherent
   group. Evaluate on a fixed labeled set with a held-out portion before another full hierarchy run.
4. Do not exempt leftover groups from validity checks merely because they were formed in the leftover
   pass. “Trim but never dissolve” is also ineffective when every child is listed as an outlier.
   Keep the pass bounded, but calibrate the judgment and choose among renaming, targeted reassignment,
   and splitting based on the actual defect.

### Additional code gaps found during verification

- **Trimmed groups can override a negative rename judgment.** In `hierarchy.resolve_groups`, the
  post-trim rename result is accepted with `coherent` overwritten to `True`. A valid output with
  `coherent: false` can therefore be concealed. Preserve and handle that result explicitly. This
  is a code-path finding; the verification did not establish that it occurred in this run.
- **Cache seeding assumes policy compatibility.** `audit.seed_cache` stamps previous results with
  the current fingerprint without verifying the previous audit's policy/model provenance. The
  inspected v3 prompt/schema match for this run, but the general seeding path should verify archived
  provenance or refuse reuse when it cannot establish compatibility.

Recommended next step: fix these two code gaps and calibrate group coherence and title faithfulness
before another hierarchy → audit → publish comparison. Retain v3 and v4 as separate reference artifacts.
