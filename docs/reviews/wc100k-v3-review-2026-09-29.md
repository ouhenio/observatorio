## Review method

Compared both publications level by level; read every v3 top-level node and its children; checked the
eight base clusters flagged in the previous review; counted `NO_FIT` answers and incoherent groups in
the L1/L2 assignment and rename logs; scanned all v3 descriptions for capitalized multi-word names as an
audit spot-check. Diagnostic sampling, not a representative evaluation.

## Findings

1. **Label defects from the previous review are resolved.** All 943 base labels passed the quality gate
   on the first generation, including the eight flagged items: `L0-0821` `Cre` → *Write detailed creative
   fiction scenes*; `L0-0377` `Unnamed cluster` → *Retrieve specific factual data and concise
   information*; the truncated descriptions of `L0-0017` and `L0-0639` are complete; the generation
   commentary in `L0-0956` is gone; the private names in `L0-0422` no longer appear. Descriptions are in
   consistent third person. Schema-constrained JSON plus the output-only label prompt appear to be what
   removed these failures; there is no ablation separating the two.

2. **The top level is fragmented.** 30 top-level categories against a target of 10. Nine multi-child
   categories hold 92.7% of conversations; 21 single-child categories hold 7,050 conversations (7.3%).
   - 16 of the 21 are one base cluster lifted twice (`NO_FIT` at L1 and again at L2), so the same name
     appears at all three levels and the viewer shows "1 children" chains.
   - Most are overlapping safety-evasion clusters that no neutral category covers: *Trigger jailbreak modes
     to bypass safety guidelines* (73), *Adopt personas to bypass safety guidelines* (108), *Adopt
     unrestricted personas or override safety constraints* (76), *Roleplay without safety constraints or
     ethical guidelines* (146), *Bypass safety filters for illegal content* (214), *Obtain aids for
     cybercrime and illegal exploitation* (85), *Obtain API keys, tokens, and account credentials* (41),
     *Obtain private personal information and credentials* (33), *Generate valid software and game license
     keys* (31), *Provocation tests and manipulation attempts* (72).
   - The rest are small leftovers with no umbrella: *Start multilingual conversational exchanges* (52),
     *Instruct assistants to memorize and recall specific information* (56), *Handle mixed requests across
     unrelated topics* (77), *Handle diverse and unrelated user inquiries* (99).
   `NO_FIT` did what it was designed for: these clusters are no longer filed under creative writing, as v2's
   `L2-002` did. But nothing regroups what it leaves out, so each leftover becomes its own top-level
   category.

3. **A top-level parent is incoherent but self-reports as coherent.** `L2-008` *Develop code, systems, and
   business strategies* (7,785) combines software development with *Develop comprehensive business
   strategies and operational plans* (1,039), *Analyze business, financial, and market data* (718) and
   *Design learning curricula, study plans, and event schedules* (435). Its rename returned
   `coherent: true`. Meanwhile the coherent mid-level coding group *Develop, extend, and debug software
   code* (1,934) was `NO_FIT` at L2 and sits beside it as a singleton. The self-reported coherence flag, set
   in the same call that writes the name, does not catch compound titles. The earlier review's rule
   against "a conjunction of unrelated topics" is stated in the prompt but not enforced.

4. **The largest category is broad.** `L2-003` *Generate diverse written and visual creative content* holds
   33,781 conversations (35.1%) over 26 children, mixing image-prompt generation (*Generate structured
   prompts for AI image creation*, 10,292, i.e. 10.7% of the corpus on its own), fiction, academic papers,
   professional correspondence, SEO copy and marketing. The level-2 proposal step returned only 9 names
   for a target of 10 (3 neighborhoods of ~44). The top level is under-divided where the data is dense and
   over-divided where it is sparse (finding 2).

5. **The audit is uncalibrated in the lenient direction.** Under the new policy (§3 of the previous review),
   1,105 of 1,106 nodes scored 5 and none scored below 4. The spot-check found 28 capitalized multi-word
   names in descriptions, all public technologies, products or events (*React Native*, *Google Sheets*,
   *Active Directory*, *Pine Script*, *World Cup*, *Doki Doki Literature Club*). None looked private. But
   the new rubric has not been validated: no labeled set shows it still catches private details. A
   near-uniform score is consistent with both "clean labels" and "rubber stamp".

6. **Fail-closed checks worked, but a rule missing from the prompt stopped a run.** The first v3 attempt
   stopped at L1 proposals ("refusing partial coverage") because category names exceeded the quality
   gate's 10-word limit. The label and rename prompts stated that limit; the proposal and dedup prompts did
   not, and the schema limited characters, not words. Fixed by stating the limit in the naming criteria and
   enforcing it with a JSON-schema `pattern` (2–10 words, no markup), which xgrammar honours (tests in
   `tests/test_schema.py`). No output was published from the failed attempt; the rerun proposed 97 names
   for a target of 97 with no retries.

7. **Carried over, not addressed in v3.** Extraction is unchanged. Instruction leakage (1,032 records
   mentioning "proper noun", 1.04%), long and multi-topic coverage, and the concern rubric (previous review
   §1, §2, §7) remain open. Base clusters and embeddings are identical to v2.

## Recommended order

1. Regroup `NO_FIT` leftovers and check parent coherence independently (items 1–2 below). Hierarchy-only
   rerun, about 10–15 minutes of GPU; labels and audit inputs are reusable.
2. Revisit level targets once 1 lands (item 3).
3. Build an audit calibration set before relying on the lenient rubric (item 4).
4. Adjust how single-child chains are presented (item 5).
5. Then the carried-over extraction work from the previous review, starting with the fixed comparison set.

Keep v3 (`serve.duckdb`, `hierarchy.parquet`) as the baseline for comparison; version the outputs as before.

## Proposed changes

### 1. Leftover pass: regroup what `NO_FIT` leaves out

**Change:** `hierarchy.build_level`. After assignment, collect the children answered `NO_FIT`. When there
are more than a few (for example, more than 3, or more than 10% of the level target), run propose →
dedup → assign again over **only the leftovers**, as a single neighborhood, with `NO_FIT` still allowed.
Add the resulting parents to the level; only clusters that are `NO_FIT` again stay lifted.

Expected effect: the ten or so safety-evasion leftovers are coherent among themselves and should form one or
two categories (e.g. *Attempt to bypass assistant safeguards*, *Obtain credentials, keys, or illicit
tools*). This follows from the evidence, so the neutral-naming rule (previous review §5) is respected. A
handful of genuinely unrelated leftovers may remain. Following the previous review, do not wrap them in an
unexplained miscellaneous bucket. Either keep them as honest singletons or group them under an explicitly
described *Other small request types* whose description lists what it contains, and record their count in
`run_meta`.

Do not remove `NO_FIT` at the top level: forcing assignment brings back v2's misfiling.

### 2. Judge coherence separately from naming

**Change:** `hierarchy.rename` / `assemble_parents`. Today the same call writes the parent name and
reports `coherent`; `L2-008` shows it can report `true` for a compound title. Add an independent check
after renaming, using the same model with a constrained `yes`/`no` answer per child: "Does the category
*<name>: <description>* truthfully cover the cluster *<child name: description>*?" Children answered `no`
are reassigned with `NO_FIT` allowed (and then flow into the leftover pass). If more than a set fraction of
a parent's children fail, treat the parent as incoherent and split it, as `assemble_parents` already does.
This is one short constrained call per child (about 1,100 at L1 + L2), cheap next to the rest of the stage.

A cheaper, weaker complement: flag parents whose child descriptions are widely dispersed in embedding space
(mean cosine to their centroid below the level's typical value) for the check above, rather than checking
every child.

### 3. Size the top level from the data

**Change:** `--top` and the level-2 proposal step. With the leftover pass in place, re-evaluate the
target: a 10-category top level puts 35% of conversations in one creative-writing category and makes
image-prompt generation (10.7% of the corpus) a child rather than a peer. Options: raise `--top` to 12–15;
or split any top-level category above a share threshold (for example 20%) by rerunning the level for its
children. Choose by reading the resulting tree, not by the count alone.

### 4. Calibrate the privacy audit

**Change:** new evaluation, no pipeline change. Build a small labeled set, in the spirit of Clio's auditor
validation (1,237 examples there). Take a few hundred v3 names/descriptions and matched variants with
injected private details (personal names, small-business names, handles, precise locations), plus public
technology and product names that should pass. Report how often the new rubric scores private variants
below 3 and public ones at 3 or above. Until then, treat the 1,105 fives as "no issues found by an
uncalibrated auditor", not as evidence of privacy.

### 5. Present single-child chains honestly

**Change:** viewer (`web/index.html`), optionally `publish.py`. A base cluster lifted to the top shows up as
three identical nodes with "1 children". Either collapse such chains in the tree (show the base cluster
once, marked *not grouped at higher levels*), or record the `NO_FIT` provenance in `hierarchy.parquet` so
the viewer can label it. The publication contract (strictly increasing levels, equal counts) is unaffected.

### 6. Keep gate rules, prompts and schemas in sync

**Change:** tests. Finding 6 came from a rule enforced in `quality.py` but absent from two prompts and the
schema. Add a test asserting that every hard name/description rule in `quality.check_name` either appears
in the naming criteria or is enforced by the schema (length, word count, markup). `tests/test_schema.py`
covers the word limit; extend it as rules change.

## Output controls that worked and should stay

- JSON-schema outputs for labels, proposals, dedup, renames and audit; constrained `choice` (IDs plus
  `NO_FIT`) for assignment. Zero invalid or cut-off answers in the successful run.
- The deterministic quality gate as a retry trigger and publish-time check, separate from the privacy audit.
- Fail-closed publication: partial coverage, incomplete labels and incoherent parents stop the stage
  instead of publishing, and an existing `serve.duckdb` is never overwritten by a failed run.

## Follow-up review comments

The review's main conclusion is sound: v3 improves output reliability, while taxonomy quality remains
the main unresolved problem. A separate read of the current publication confirmed 943 base, 133 middle,
and 30 top-level clusters, each level totaling 96,324 conversations. This check confirms those structural
figures, not every semantic judgment in the review.

1. **Regroup leftovers before falling back to singletons.** The singleton fallback preserves coverage,
   but this run demonstrates its fragmentation cost. Add one bounded regrouping pass over `NO_FIT`
   children, retain `NO_FIT` during that pass, and preserve any remaining unmatched children as
   singletons. Avoid an unbounded propose/assign/review loop or forcing leftovers into a miscellaneous
   category merely to reduce the top-level count.

2. **Check whole-group coherence as well as child-to-parent fit.** A title such as “Develop code,
   systems, and business strategies” can truthfully cover each child while still describing a poor
   grouping. The proposed per-child yes/no check alone would not necessarily catch this. Add a separate
   group-level judgment using all child names and descriptions: do they share a meaningful activity at
   this level of abstraction, or does the title merely enumerate unrelated activities? Request specific
   outlier child IDs alongside that judgment so repairs can target assignments. A separate call to the
   same model remains a heuristic with correlated errors, not independent validation of correctness.

3. **Rerun the parent audit after hierarchy changes.** Unchanged leaf labels and their audit results can
   be reused. Newly generated parent names/descriptions require another audit, followed by publication.
   Do not reuse audit scores by cluster ID alone: a hierarchy rerun can assign the same ID to different
   content. Any audit cache should be tied to the exact audited content and audit policy/model version.
   Thus the recommended rerun is hierarchy → parent audit → publish, rather than hierarchy alone.

4. **Use category size as a review trigger, not an automatic split rule.** A category containing 35% of
   conversations could accurately reflect the dataset. Split when its children represent distinct,
   useful activities; image prompting versus professional correspondence is a plausible example here.
   Evaluate the repaired hierarchy before increasing `--top` or introducing a share threshold. Category
   count alone is not a quality metric.

5. **Qualify the audit finding.** “Uncalibrated in the lenient direction” is stronger than the evidence
   establishes. The demonstrated result is an uncalibrated auditor with near-uniform scores; excessive
   leniency has not yet been demonstrated. The proposed matched-example evaluation is appropriate.
   Include indirect identifying combinations (role, organization, location, unusual event) as well as
   explicit private names and identifiers, and measure missed private details separately from rejection
   of legitimate public technologies. Treat the current scores as “no issues found by an uncalibrated
   auditor.”

6. **Group sensitive requests by action, not just their objects.** Credentials, API keys, and license
   questions are not automatically illicit. Inspect the actual requested action before combining these
   leftovers under a cybercrime or exploitation label. Legitimate authentication setup, account recovery,
   and requests to obtain someone else's credentials should not acquire the same implied intent merely
   because they mention similar objects.

Recommended next experiment: one bounded leftover regrouping pass plus group-level coherence evaluation,
then audit the changed parents, republish, and compare the resulting tree against v3. Defer changing the
top-level target until that comparison shows what remains fragmented or overly broad.
